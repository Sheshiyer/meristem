# Cambium — Visual System

## Creative idea: the Living Ledger

Cambium's visual language joins two truths: a venture is alive, and consequential decisions need a record. Organic fields, growth rings, cambium layers, seasonal light, branching paths, and living workspaces express adaptation. Ledger lines, boundary marks, evidence cards, calibrated diagrams, and visible gates express accountability.

The result should feel alive but not mystical, technical but not sterile, and premium without using luxury theatre.

## Palette

- **Night Soil** — near-black mineral ground for depth and legibility.
- **Cambium Green** — restrained living accent for viable movement and renewal.
- **Mineral Blue** — evidence, reflection, and reviewable structure.
- **Seasonal Amber** — attention, energy, and a boundary approaching review.
- **Bone Paper** — warm light ground for long-form reading and physical artifacts.
- **Signal Red** — reserved for violated bounds, holds, and consequential warnings.

Green does not mean blanket permission. Red should not be used decoratively.

## Typography

The wordmark and editorial layer should use a thoughtful serif with visible craft and good long-form rhythm. Interface, metadata, and diagrams should use a neutral grotesk or humanist sans with strong numerical clarity. Monospaced type is reserved for receipts, identifiers, and replay records.

Hierarchy should feel like a field guide: generous titles, concise section markers, readable body text, and quiet evidence labels.

## Image direction

Photography shows real stewardship: people in lived workspaces, daylight changing across a desk, evidence laid out before a decision, and moments of pause or review. Subjects are not depicted as triumphant founders commanding a machine. The atmosphere is attentive, grounded, and capable.

Product imagery uses conceptual objects such as a Steward Kit, decision cards, field ledgers, boundary tokens, and seasonal maps. These are explicitly conceptual visualizations, not proof of manufactured hardware or a shipping product.

Illustration uses contour fields, nested boundaries, rings, branches, and small operator figures tending more than one garden. Avoid generic neural-network imagery, robots, glowing brains, dashboards floating in space, and corporate handshake scenes.

## Asset families

The current visual sourcebook includes:

- a living-boundary logo symbol;
- two wide hero fields: the continuity studio and viable field;
- founder and portfolio stewardship photography;
- a conceptual Steward Kit product scene;
- square and wide social compositions;
- illustrations for one operator tending many gardens and finite instruments serving infinite purpose;
- an icon-system reference sheet;
- a contour-field pattern master.

The contour-field master is a source motif, not a verified seamless tile. Every conceptual asset should carry a clear label when shown in a context that could be mistaken for product proof.

## Accessibility and restraint

Color cannot be the only carrier of status. Boundary states also need words, symbols, or patterns. Text should retain readable contrast over imagery. Motion should be slow, optional, and meaningful; the system must remain understandable without animation.

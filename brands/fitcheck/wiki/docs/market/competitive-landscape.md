# Market and competitive landscape

## Category context

Shopify's virtual-try-on category displayed 297 apps when accessed on
2026-08-10. That indicates a crowded directory, not a market-size estimate or
evidence of demand. For Fitcheck, it makes deliberate distribution and distinct
positioning more important than assuming App Store search will create early
adoption.

Shopify also identifies fit and sizing difficulty as a common driver of returns
in apparel and footwear. This supports fit uncertainty as a merchant problem to
explore. It does **not** prove that Fitcheck reduces returns.

## Direct name and category competitor

Synthenova has a public Shopify App Store listing named
[FitCheck | Virtual Try-On](https://apps.shopify.com/fitcheck-1). The source
ledger records a zero-review observation and free/usage-based plans from the
retrieval date; live App Store fields can change and should be rechecked before
any public comparison.

The overlap makes name collision a practical discoverability and differentiation
constraint. Fitcheck retains its name while using the descriptor **AI Fit
Confidence for Shopify** and distinct language and visual identity. This is a
positioning choice, not a claim of legal clearance or a technical moat.

## Alternatives a merchant may consider

| Alternative | What it offers | Evidence-bounded limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard product photography, fit notes, and size charts | Familiar merchandising control and product context. | A shopper may still need to infer how a garment could look on them. |
| Generic virtual try-on listing | A category-level try-on interaction and App Store distribution. | Do not assert category-wide feature or quality gaps without direct evidence. |
| Custom try-on project | A potentially tailored experience with internal or agency ownership. | It can require greater evaluation, implementation, and maintenance capacity. This is a product-choice hypothesis, not a universal rule. |

## Working differentiation hypothesis

Fitcheck's early territory is a credible, fashion-aware product-page decision
moment: a focused AI try-on rollout, guided merchant learning, and explicit
limits instead of generic AI spectacle or promised commercial outcomes.

This must be tested with merchants. It is not yet a proven competitive advantage.

## Distribution learning

Internal outreach observations provide a direction for further tests: Fashion
Marketplaces had the strongest recorded reply-rate cohort (3.0%), while Retail
Tech Teams and Shopify Fashion Brands each supplied two provider-labelled hot
leads. These are small operational signals, not statistically conclusive ICP or
product-market-fit findings.

The practical next test is targeted outreach plus a focused merchant pilot, with
merchant-specific measurement and documented consent before outcomes become
public proof.

# Claims and evidence boundaries

Fitcheck's public language should explain a useful product-page visual
experience without turning it into an unmeasured business or fit guarantee.

## Supported language

Current evidence supports these bounded statements:

- Fitcheck is an AI try-on experience for Shopify fashion product pages.
- It gives shoppers a more personal visual way to explore a garment while they
  decide.
- Fitcheck is positioned as **AI Fit Confidence for Shopify**.
- Merchants can begin with a focused product set and evaluate the experience in
  their own storefront.
- The approved price is **$99 monthly** or **$799 yearly**.

## Language that needs Fitcheck-specific proof

Do not publish claims about conversion lift, revenue lift, return reduction,
adoption volume, customer satisfaction, or particular merchant outcomes until a
dated Fitcheck-specific receipt exists and its approved scope is recorded.

Do not claim that a try-on result guarantees a garment's size, fit, appearance,
or purchase suitability. A visual try-on can support exploration; it cannot
replace a shopper's judgment or the merchant's product and fit information.

## Privacy and platform boundaries

Shopify's public guidance calls for truthful, privacy-safe apps and for
minimization and transparency around protected customer data. The current ledger
does not establish Fitcheck's data classification, retention practice, consent
flow, or security posture. Do not make those claims without an audited product
and privacy review.

## Internal signals are not customer proof

Existing outreach analytics are useful for prioritizing further learning. They
are not proof of product-market fit, merchant demand, revenue, or product
outcomes. Keep internal operating observations separate from customer-facing
claims.

# Mission and values

Fitcheck is building an AI try-on experience for Shopify fashion product pages.
Its purpose is to give shoppers a more informed visual decision moment when
standard product imagery leaves a personal fit question open. That framing is
intentional: a visual try-on can support exploration, but it does not establish
sizing certainty, purchase suitability, or a commercial outcome.

## Mission

> Help fashion shoppers see a garment in a more personal context before they
> decide.

For merchants, the corresponding job is to make the product page more
considered without treating an adoption decision as an open-ended custom
technology project. Each merchant should validate value in its own storefront.

## Vision

The long-term direction is a fashion-commerce experience in which a shopper has
more than a flat product image when deciding what to buy. This is a brand
aspiration, not a claim that Fitcheck can show every garment on every body or
eliminate shopping hesitation.

## Values

### Fit truth over fit guessing

Describe the try-on as a more informed visual starting point. Do not represent
it as proof of fit, exact sizing, fabric behaviour, or a guaranteed result.

### Merchant-first speed

Give merchants a clear, bounded path to test the experience. Never imply a
setup time or business outcome unless a dated Fitcheck-specific receipt supports
it.

### Body realism, not body idealism

Treat representation as a product and design responsibility. Show people with
respect, avoid a single aspirational silhouette, and do not use images to make
claims about a result's fidelity.

### Pragmatic precision

Use clear Shopify and product-page language. Prefer merchant-specific learning
over broad promises about conversion, returns, revenue, or adoption.

## Decision checks

Before publishing a page, campaign, or design, ask:

1. Does it help a shopper or merchant understand a more informed visual
   decision—not a guaranteed one?
2. Does it turn an aspiration into a measurable promise without evidence?
3. Does it respect the limitations, privacy questions, and body context of a
   shopper-photo experience?

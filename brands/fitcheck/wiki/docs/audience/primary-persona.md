# Primary persona: Operator Aisha

> **Research hypothesis, not customer research.** “Operator Aisha” is a
> planning device to guide merchant interviews, campaign learning, and product
> decisions. It does not describe a verified Fitcheck customer, market segment,
> or demographic fact.

## Working profile

Aisha is a founder or ecommerce lead at an emerging to mid-market Shopify
fashion brand. She is accountable for a product page that reflects the
collection, earns shopper confidence, and does not create an unnecessary
implementation burden for a lean team.

The existing persona draft models her as an urban US or UK operator in her
thirties. Those demographic details are assumptions to test; they must not be
used for targeting exclusions or represented as market evidence.

## Jobs to be done

1. Help shoppers form a more personal visual impression when standard imagery
   leaves a fit question open.
2. Protect a deliberate brand experience while trying a new product-page
   interaction.
3. Run a focused test before expanding a technology or merchandising change.

## Hypothesised tensions

| Tension | Current workaround | Fitcheck learning question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product imagery can be clear but impersonal. | More model, flat-lay, detail photography, size charts, and fit notes. | Does a try-on interaction make the decision feel more informed for this store's shoppers? |
| Fit uncertainty makes a purchase decision feel risky. | Customer-support guidance and merchandising copy. | Which questions can a visual interaction responsibly help explore? |
| A new tool competes with collection and launch work. | Custom build, long evaluation, or doing nothing. | Can a bounded rollout create enough merchant-specific evidence to justify a next step? |
| A generic widget can feel misaligned. | Avoid try-on or accept a broadly positioned app. | What visual, privacy, and operational conditions make the experience feel native to the brand? |

## Signals and validation plan

Internal outbound observations suggest fashion marketplaces had the strongest
reply-rate cohort (3.0%) and that Retail Tech Teams and Shopify Fashion Brands
each produced two provider-labelled hot leads. These are small operating
signals, not proof of an ICP or demand. Use them to prioritise interviews, then
record who was interviewed, the question asked, date, consent, and the exact
evidence before changing this page.

Start each interview by testing the problem, not the product pitch:

- Where does a shopper still hesitate after reviewing the product page?
- What would make a try-on moment feel useful rather than distracting?
- What privacy, consent, brand, and implementation questions would need clear
  answers before a trial?
- Which merchant-owned measurement would determine whether to expand?

## Message implications

Address this audience with a precise, fashion-aware, practical voice. Lead with
a more informed product-page decision and a focused learning path; never lead
with a conversion, return-reduction, sizing, or speed guarantee.
